THREE
DECISIONS

1

IMAGE AND AUDIO BEFORE TEXT

Learners could not rely on long reading. We gave up detailed written explanations.

2

NARRATIVE SIMULATION, NOT FORUMS

Low connectivity. We gave up live peer discussion; the course prepares people for their real group.

3

VALIDATE WITH THE COMMUNITY FIRST

Trust was the real gap. It took time, and it was worth it.

HOW I MEASURE

I turn learning data into experiments and decide from what they show.

LOOP 1 · WHAT IS WORTH TESTINGLOOP 2 · TEST IT AND DECIDE
1MONITORwhere a course stalls
2DIAGNOSEone hypothesis
3DESIGNbrief and decision rule
4RUNwith the people involved
5EVALUATEnumbers and words
6DECIDEadopt, adjust, repeat, abandon

↺ THE LEARNING LOG FEEDS THE NEXT QUESTION

Before launching, I write down the hypothesis and the decision rule, which tell me in advance whether to adopt, adjust, repeat or abandon the course. Afterwards I read the numbers together with the participants' own words and log every result, including the ones that do not confirm the idea.

Beyond completion, I look at what participants do afterwards: with Python scrapers that query public APIs, I check who went on to edit Wikipedia after a course, and I use the points where learners drop off to test my hypothesis that a sense of community keeps people learning.

All of it runs in reproducible Jupyter notebooks, as part of a v0.1 pilot that I proposed to my department, called Learning Experimentation & Impact Evaluation, in which every number carries its unit, denominator, source and date.

HOW I WOULD CARRY IT

UNDERSTAND

I would write the learner profile and the constraints into the course brief, so that every later decision can be checked against them.

ORGANISE

I would organise the content into modules with ordered pages and completion requirements, and I would build a reusable course template.

GIVE FORM

I would design media-first pages and scenario quizzes with feedback, and I would make everything accessible by default.

ASK FOR EVIDENCE

I would link learning outcomes and rubrics to each activity, so that the evidence of learning is built into the course itself.

DELIVER AND LEARN

Before publishing I would run a review checklist, and I would keep a decision log and Blueprint-style templates for the next course.

WHAT I LEARNED

1

Always have a method and clear ideas.

2

Think about the learner: how they think, and how they learn.

3

Make it friendly and engaging. Knowledge should not be boring; it should be accessible.
